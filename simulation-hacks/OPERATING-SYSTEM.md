## The reframe: your bottleneck is not knowledge, it's executive function

Every artifact in this repo pointed at the same gap — you consume elite information and act on almost none of it. Now we know *why*, and it isn't a character flaw. If you're low-COMT ("worrier" genotype: slow dopamine clearance, high baseline prefrontal dopamine), your working memory is actually good **when calm** — but prefrontal dopamine runs on an inverted-U, and you already sit near the top of it. Stress, sleep debt, a chaotic house, a deadline, two kids needing things at once — each one pushes you *over* the peak into the exact distractible, can't-filter, can't-initiate state that looks like classic inattention. Your saved Chris Williamson snip ("COMT Met Met Shapes Reaction And Strengths") describes this profile in his own words.

The strategic consequence is large: **the answer is never "try harder" or "consume more."** Willpower and information are the two levers that don't work for your hardware. The levers that *do* work are (1) protecting a calm window, (2) externalizing executive function into the environment, and (3) defending sleep. Everything below is one of those three.

## Keystone move: turn the commute into a capture engine

Your commute is 1.5–2.3 hours a day of the one thing your life almost never gives you — a solo, uninterrupted block. Right now it's passive podcast intake, which is dopamine-cheap and quietly reinforces the consume-don't-act loop. You don't need to give up the podcasts. You need to add one near-zero-effort output step, because output is the entire thing that's missing.

**The move:** hands-free voice capture, one per drive.
- **Morning drive = capture.** Before or after the episode, say one thing out loud into a voice memo (or a transcribing app): one *glitch* you noticed, or one *next action* you'll take. That's it. It transcribes; you paste it into `glitches.md` or your task list later — or have an AI do it. This single habit makes the entire playbook run without carving out any new time, because it rides on time you already spend.
- **Why it beats "sit down and journal":** it requires zero initiation energy (you're already in the car, hands occupied, no blank page to face — the two things ADD hates most: starting, and staring at blankness). You're just talking.

If you do only one thing from this whole session, do this. It converts your biggest passive block into your only reliable output channel.

## Protect the calm peak (the inverted-U rule)

For your hardware, *when* you do demanding work matters more than how much. Hard creative/analytic work belongs in your calmest, most-rested window — usually morning, before stress accumulates — **not** at 9pm after bedtime when you're depleted and already over the peak. High-COMT "warrior" types do their best work under pressure; you are likely the opposite, so grinding-when-fried is actively counterproductive for you, not just unpleasant.

- Claim one protected ~30-min morning block for the *one* build project (from your player notes), before the day's stress loads in.
- Treat the depleted evening as recovery time, not a second shift. Trying to be productive then costs more than it returns.

## The evening commute = your arrival airlock

You deliberately saved fatherhood snips about arriving present, not depleted (the John Schott "arrival routine" material). Your low-COMT stress-sensitivity is exactly why this matters more for you: you walk in the door already near the top of the curve, and a chaotic house tips you over — so you snap or check out. Build the airlock:
- **Evening drive = decompression, not more input.** No hard podcasts on the way home. Music, silence, or one deliberate "leave work here" ritual. The commute is the transition; use it as one.
- **A fixed 5–10 min at the door** (sit in the driveway, a walk around the block, phone parked) before you go in. You already consume Huberman-grade protocols; this is the one your own saved data says you need.

## Externalize executive function (stop trusting your working memory)

Low-COMT-under-load means ideas evaporate fast and initiation is expensive. Don't fight it — move the load out of your head and into the environment:
- **Capture anywhere, instantly.** The voice memo above, a single notes inbox, whatever — the rule is *never* rely on remembering. If it isn't captured in 10 seconds, it's gone.
- **One visible next action, not a project.** ADD can't start "build the thing"; it can start "open the file and write one function." Always reduce the next step until it's stupidly small.
- **Reduce house/life surface area** (this is the "minimal surface area" glitch applied at home): fewer decisions, more standing defaults and routines. Every recurring decision you can automate or template is prefrontal load you free up for the stuff that matters.
- **Body-double with the kids.** Your 10-year-old does homework at the table; you do your 20-minute build *next to them*. Co-located focus ("body doubling") is one of the most reliable ADD hacks, and it doubles as presence — you're there, not hiding in another room.

## The basics that actually move your curve

These sit *upstream* of every supplement, and for your genotype they're not optional:
- **Sleep is the dominant variable.** Sleep deprivation directly degrades prefrontal dopamine function — it shoves you off the inverted-U before the day even starts. Creatine only *partially* rescues a sleep-deprived brain; it does not replace sleep. With a commute and two kids this is your hardest and highest-leverage lever. You've saved sleep episodes (Attia #394 pharmacology, Huberman sleep-tech) but no distilled protocol — worth building one page you actually follow.
- **Creatine: keep it, understand it.** Your "it helps somewhat" is mechanistically real — it buffers the phosphocreatine/ATP system the energy-hungry prefrontal cortex leans on, and the evidence is strongest exactly under *sleep debt and stress*, which is your life. You've got the source material (Candow episode #382, Solosode #499 noting working-memory/mental-fatigue RCTs under sleep deprivation). It's a legit adjunct for buffering a strained system — not a fix for the strain. Dosing: your clinician, not this doc.
- **Aerobic exercise** reliably improves the next several hours of executive function (BDNF + catecholamine tone). Even a short morning bout buys you a better calm-peak window. Your exercise-for-cognition material is thin — a gap worth filling.

## The one-week version

1. **Tomorrow's drive:** one voice memo, one glitch or one action. Repeat daily. (Keystone — everything else is optional next to this.)
2. **This week:** claim one protected 30-min morning block for the single build project.
3. **This week:** one fixed decompression ritual before walking in the door.
4. **Ongoing:** capture instantly, shrink every next-step until it's trivial, and defend sleep like it's the load-bearing wall — because for your hardware, it is.

You don't have an information problem or a discipline problem. You have a system that was tuned for someone else's brain. Retune it for yours and the leverage was there the whole time.
